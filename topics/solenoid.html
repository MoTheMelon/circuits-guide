Solenoid & flyback diode

A solenoid is a coil that pulls a steel plunger when energised — door locks, valves, pinball flippers, recoil mechanisms. It draws a lot of current (often 0.5–2 A) and stores real energy in its magnetic field, so the driver circuit is all about switching that energy safely.

I = V / Rcoil
Steady current. A 12 V, 20 Ω solenoid draws 0.6 A, 7.2 W.
F ∝ (N I)²
Pull force rises with the square of ampere-turns and falls steeply as the air gap grows.
v = L didt
At switch-off the coil generates whatever voltage keeps its current flowing.

Required circuit

  • N-channel logic-level MOSFETLow side, rated for ≥ 2× the solenoid current and supply voltage.
  • Flyback diode across the coilCathode to +V, anode to the MOSFET drain. 1N4001–1N4007 or a Schottky (SS34) rated for the coil current.
  • Gate resistor + pull-down100 Ω series, 10 kΩ gate-to-ground.
  • Bulk capacitor100–1000 µF across the supply near the solenoid so the inrush doesn't brown out your microcontroller. Share grounds, but run the solenoid's current return separately.
  • Duty cycleMany solenoids are rated for intermittent duty; holding them on continuously overheats them. PWM-reduce the hold current if needed.
