Operational amplifier (op-amp)

An op-amp amplifies the difference between its two inputs by an enormous gain (~100 000×). On its own it slams to a rail; with negative feedback from output to the − input it settles wherever the inputs are equal, giving precise, resistor-defined gain.

Rule 1: V+ = V−
With negative feedback, the output does whatever makes the inputs equal.
Rule 2: Iin = 0
No current flows into the inputs.
G = 1 + RfRg
Non-inverting amplifier.
G = −RfRin
Inverting amplifier; input impedance = Rin.

Every op-amp needs power pins (±V or single supply + GND) with a 100 nF decoupling cap each. "Rail-to-rail" parts (MCP6002, TLV9002) swing close to the supplies and suit 3.3 V systems; older parts like the LM358 cannot reach the positive rail. For comparators, a dedicated comparator IC (LM393) with a little positive feedback (hysteresis) beats an op-amp.
