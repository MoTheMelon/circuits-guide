Switches, pull-ups & debouncing

A switch only connects or disconnects; a digital input needs a defined voltage in both states. A pull-up resistor holds the pin HIGH and the button pulls it to ground (reads LOW when pressed); a pull-down does the reverse. Without one, the pin "floats" and picks up noise.

Most microcontrollers have internal pull-ups (~50 kΩ; the Pico's are ~50–80 kΩ) you can enable in software — e.g. Pin(15, Pin.IN, Pin.PULL_UP) in MicroPython. External 4.7–10 kΩ pull-ups are stiffer against noise on long wires.

Contact bounce

Mechanical contacts physically bounce for a few milliseconds, producing a burst of edges. An RC low-pass followed by a Schmitt-trigger input (with two thresholds) turns that burst into one clean edge.

τ = RC ≈ 5–20 ms
Long enough to swallow bounce, short enough to feel instant.
