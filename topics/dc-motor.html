DC motor & H-bridge

A brushed DC motor spins at a speed roughly proportional to its voltage, and draws current proportional to the torque it delivers. While spinning it acts as a generator (back-EMF), which is why current is huge at startup or stall. To reverse it you swap the voltage polarity — the job of an H-bridge.

V = I Ra + ke ω
Applied voltage = winding drop + back-EMF.
Istall = V / Ra
Size drivers and supplies for stall current, often 5–10× the running current.
T = kt I
Torque is proportional to current.

For one direction only, a single low-side MOSFET with a flyback diode across the motor is enough (same circuit as the solenoid). Add a 100 nF ceramic directly across the motor terminals to suppress brush noise. Speed control is by PWM.
