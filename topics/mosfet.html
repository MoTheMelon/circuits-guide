MOSFET

A MOSFET is a voltage-controlled switch: the gate is insulated, so (apart from brief charging current) it draws no steady current. When VGS exceeds the threshold, a channel forms between drain and source with a small resistance RDS(on). N-channel parts are the default low-side switch for motors, solenoids, LED strips and heaters; P-channel parts switch the high side.

ID = ½k(VGS − Vth)²
Saturation (current-limited) region, square-law model.
ID = k[(VGS−Vth)VDS − ½VDS²]
Ohmic / triode region, when VDS < VGS − Vth.
P = ID² · RDS(on)
Heat when fully on. A 20 mΩ part at 5 A dissipates 0.5 W.

What a real low-side MOSFET switch needs

  • Logic-level partVGS(th) is where it starts to conduct, not where it's fully on. From a 3.3 V GPIO, pick a part whose datasheet gives RDS(on) at VGS = 2.5 V (e.g. AO3400, IRLML6344, IRLZ44N is marginal at 3.3 V).
  • Gate resistor 47–220 ΩLimits the inrush current into the gate capacitance and damps ringing.
  • Gate pull-down 10–100 kΩHolds the MOSFET off while the microcontroller boots and its pin floats.
  • Flyback diode on inductive loadsAcross the load, cathode to +V. See solenoid.
