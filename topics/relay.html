Relay

A relay is an electromagnet that mechanically moves a switch contact. It gives complete electrical isolation: a 5 V coil can switch a separate high-voltage or AC load. The coil is an inductor, so it needs the same driver treatment as a solenoid: a transistor (GPIOs can't supply ~70 mA) and a flyback diode.

  • Coil currentI = Vcoil/Rcoil; a typical 5 V hobby relay is 70 Ω → ~70 mA.
  • Contact ratingCheck both voltage and current, AC and DC separately; DC arcs are harder to break.
  • ContactsCOM (common), NO (normally open), NC (normally closed).
  • AlternativesSolid-state relays (SSRs) have no moving parts and no coil kick, but leak a little current and drop ~1 V.
