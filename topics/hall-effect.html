Hall-effect sensor

A Hall sensor measures magnetic field. Current flowing through a thin semiconductor plate is pushed sideways by a perpendicular field, creating a tiny voltage that the chip amplifies. Three flavours: unipolar switches (on near one pole), latches (on with S, off with N) and linear sensors (voltage proportional to field). Uses: contactless buttons and triggers, RPM sensing, end-stops, reload / magazine detection, joysticks, current sensing.

VH = I Bn q t
Hall voltage across the plate (microvolts; the IC amplifies it).
Vout = Vcc2 + S · B
Linear ratiometric sensor. S ≈ 1–5 mV/G (10–50 mV/mT). 1 mT = 10 gauss.
B ∝ 1/d³
On-axis field of a small magnet falls off roughly with the cube of distance.

What each type needs

  • Digital (switch / latch)Open-drain output: the chip can only pull OUT low. Add a 10 kΩ pull-up to the MCU's supply (or enable the internal pull-up). OUT reads LOW when the magnet is present.
  • LinearWire OUT straight to an ADC pin. Optional RC filter (1 kΩ + 100 nF). Keep the output within the ADC's range — a 5 V sensor into a 3.3 V ADC needs a divider.
  • Decoupling100 nF from VCC to GND right at the sensor pins.
  • OrientationOnly the field perpendicular to the marked face counts. Check which pole the datasheet expects on which face.
