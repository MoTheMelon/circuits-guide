Inductor & RL circuits

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field around a coil. It is the mirror image of a capacitor: it opposes changes in current. Inductors appear in switching regulators, filters, and — very importantly — in every coil-driven part: relays, solenoids and motors.

v = L didt
Voltage is proportional to how fast current changes. Current can't jump instantly.
τ = LR
RL time constant.
i(t) = VR(1 − e−t/τ)
Current build-up after switch-on.
E = ½ L I²
Stored energy — it must go somewhere at switch-off.
XL = 2πf L
Reactance: short at DC, blocks high frequencies.
Why inductors are dangerous to switch.

If you suddenly open the circuit, di/dt becomes huge, so v = L·di/dt spikes to hundreds of volts. That "inductive kick" kills transistors. See solenoid for the fix (flyback diode).
