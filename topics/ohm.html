Ohm's law & power

Voltage (V, volts) is the electrical "pressure" difference between two points. Current (I, amps) is the rate charge flows past a point. Resistance (R, ohms Ω) is how strongly a part opposes that flow. Ohm's law ties the three together, and power tells you how much energy turns into heat every second.

V = I · R
Ohm's law. Also I = V/R and R = V/I.
P = V · I
Power in watts, for any component.
P = I²R = V²R
Power in a resistor — use whichever two quantities you know.
E = P · t
Energy in joules. Battery capacity: mAh × V ÷ 1000 = Wh.
Practical rule.

Size a resistor's power rating at least 2× the power it will dissipate. Common through-hole resistors are ¼ W; 0603 SMD parts are about 0.1 W.
