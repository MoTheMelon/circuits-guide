AC signals, RMS & impedance

An AC signal is described by its amplitude, frequency and phase. RMS is the DC voltage that would deliver the same heating power — wall outlets are quoted in RMS (120 V RMS ≈ 170 V peak). Impedance Z extends resistance to AC: capacitors and inductors resist current differently at different frequencies and shift its phase.

v(t) = Vp sin(2πf t + φ)
Sinusoid; ω = 2πf in rad/s; period T = 1/f.
Vrms = Vp√2 ≈ 0.707 Vp
Sine only. Square wave: Vrms = Vp. Triangle: Vp/√3.
ZR = R
Resistor: no phase shift.
ZC = 1jωC
Capacitor: |Z| falls with frequency; current leads voltage by 90°.
ZL = jωL
Inductor: |Z| rises with frequency; current lags voltage by 90°.
V = I · Z
Ohm's law with complex numbers; series/parallel rules apply to Z.
